9.1
Magnetic Properties of Spacecraft and Their Effects
When it comes to the magnetic properties of spacecraft, the main consideration is
the constant steady magnetic ﬁeld or quasi-steady magnetic ﬁeld generated by the
spacecraft (DC magnetic ﬁeld or very low-frequency magnetic ﬁeld) with a range of
0–100 Hz. The magnetic ﬁeld in this frequency range may affect the attitude of the
spacecraft and the test accuracy of the magnetic sensitive instruments.
9.1.1
Affecting Spacecraft Attitude
The magnetic materials used in spacecraft and the operating currents may generate
a certain magnetic moment in the spacecraft. The interaction between the magnetic
moment and the magnetic ﬁeld environment will produce magnetic interference
torque on the spacecraft, thus affecting the attitude of the spacecraft.
The magnetic interference torque of the on-orbit spacecraft can be expressed as:
L = M · B
(9.1.1)
where L is the interference torque generated by the interaction between the magnetic
moment of the spacecraft and the space magnetic ﬁeld, in N m;
M is the magnetic moment of the spacecraft, in A m2;
B is the space magnetic ﬁeld, in T.
During the long on-orbit time of the spacecraft, the accumulated interaction of
the geomagnetic ﬁeld and its own magnetic moment will have a considerable impact
on the attitude of the spacecraft. For example, for a spin-stabilized spacecraft, the
magnetic interference torque will affect the control accuracy of the spacecraft atti-
tude. The torque component along the spin axis of the spacecraft produces a torque
with an average value not equal to zero in the spin cycle, causing the precession of

(1) For a spacecraft operating in a LEO magnetic environment, the magnetic inter-

of the spacecraft shall be strictly controlled;
(2) For a spacecraft operating in a MEO magnetic environment, the magnetic
interference torque has certain inﬂuence on attitude control, and the magnetic
moment of the spacecraft should be controlled;
(3) For a spacecraft operating in GEO magnetic environment, the magnetic inter-
ference torque has less inﬂuence on attitude control; only those long-life,
high-reliability satellites have certain magnetic moment control requirements;
(4) For a spacecraft operating in interstellar orbit, the magnetic interference torque
has no effect on attitude control (except for detectors with a strong magnetic
ﬁeld, such as Jupiter and the Sun).
9.1.2
Affecting the Test Accuracy of Spacecraft Magnetic
Sensitive Instruments
When the spacecraft carries magnetic ﬁeld sensitive equipment such as a magne-
tometer, the spacecraft’s own magnetic ﬁeld noise intensity must be controlled in
order to ensure the normal operation of the equipment and the validity of the magnetic
ﬁeld measurement data. In addition, for spacecraft using the magnetic torque for atti-
tude control and orbital positioning, because the strong magnetic ﬁeld generated by
the magnetic torque will affect the surrounding electronic equipment, and even affect
the health of astronauts, control measures must be taken about the strong magnetic
ﬁeld. The magnetic ﬁeld shall be controlled in the following three types of spacecraft:
(1) For scientiﬁc exploration satellites with magnetic ﬁeld detection missions, very
strict control requirements for their own magnetic ﬁelds are raised. Generally,

9.2.1
Magnetic Materials
Magnetic materials are the primary sources of spacecraft magnetism, which can
be further divided into hard magnetic materials, soft magnetic materials, and weak
magnetic materials.
The spacecraft equipment using hard magnetic materials includes relays, traveling
wave tubes, magnetic recorders, isolators, motors, ﬂywheels, gyros, solenoid valves,
transformers, and sensors.
Equipment using soft magnetic materials such as iron and iron alloys includes all
inductive devices, electrical connectors, fasteners, electronic components, nickel-
based batteries, gears, bearings, supports, metal seals, attitude control engine,
magnetic moment device, etc.

9.2.2
Working Current
The working current is also a source of spacecraft magnetism. With the change of the
operational state of the spacecraft, not only the amplitude of the magnetic moment,
but also the direction of the magnetic moment changes signiﬁcantly, which has a
particularly serious impact on attitude control. The sources of working current are
as follows:
(1) Inside the component wiring;
(2) Transformers, inductors, and coils in the inductor;
(3) Ground loop current in the device;
(4) Battery array wires;
(5) Spiral tube;
(6) Current path caused by thermocouples;
(7) Satellite-board cables;
(8) Other operating currents.

9.3.1
Determination of System Speciﬁcations
The magnetic speciﬁcations of a spacecraft mainly include magnetic moment
speciﬁcations and magnetic ﬁeld speciﬁcations.
Most spacecraft, especially MEO and LEO spacecraft, have magnetic moment
speciﬁcations for the attitude control system. According to the magnitude of the
magnetic interference moment and the attitude control level of the spacecraft, China’s
spacecraft generally have the following system-level magnetic moment control
requirements:
a.
For spacecraft operating in LEO magnetic environment, such as CAST968 and
CAST2000 platform small satellites, the magnetic moment of the whole satellite
is no more than 1 A m2; for large satellites, it is generally no more than 5 A m2;
b. For satellites operating in MEO magnetic environment, the magnetic moment of
the whole satellite is generally no more than 5 A m2; for large platforms, it is
generally no more than 15 A m2;

For satellites operating in GEO magnetic environment, no magnetic moment

For scientiﬁc exploration spacecraft, especially those for space magnetic ﬁeld

ﬁeld measurement, the magnetic cleanliness requirements are generally based on the
measurement accuracy requirements of the magnetometer. In addition, for plasma
measurement spacecraft, since the magnetic ﬁeld of the satellite itself affects the
plasma distribution around it, its magnetic cleanliness is based on the measurement
requirements of these payloads. The spacecraft magnetic ﬁeld speciﬁcations gener-

requirements, and the induced magnetic ﬁeld requirements.
Take the US magnetic exploration satellite project—the Magnetic MultiScale
(MMS) mission—as an example; it is mainly used to investigate and study the rela-
tionship between the solar magnetic ﬁeld and the Earth’s magnetic ﬁeld, that is, the
“magnetic reconnection” phenomenon. The purpose of the mission is to make an
accurate measurement of the magnetic ﬁeld in the plasma, which may be interfered
with by the magnetic ﬁeld of the spacecraft itself. At the same time, the spacecraft
magnetic ﬁeld may also affect the measurement of other loads except the magne-
tometers. To this end, the magnetic ﬁeld generated by the spacecraft can neither
affect the space magnetic ﬁeld nor affect the orbit of the electron (the direction of
its arrival is very important for the other two devices). Due to the high precision
measurement requirements of the magnetic ﬁeld between the two pairs of MMS
satellites, the magnetic ﬁeld interference needs to be very stable. Moreover, in order
to ensure that the electron orbit is not affected by the magnetic ﬁeld of the satellite,
the maximum magnetic ﬁeld interference around the satellite body must be strictly
limited. MMS satellites have a magnetic cleanliness requirement for the following
three devices: the ﬂuxgate magnetometer, the electronic drift instrument (EDI), and
electronic sensors (DES). The speciﬁc speciﬁcations are shown in Table 9.1.

9.4.2
Spacecraft Magnetic Field Simulation Method
9.4.2.1
Overview
The magnetic ﬁeld consideration in the magnetic design of a spacecraft mainly refers
to the stable magnetic ﬁeld, that is, the DC magnetic ﬁeld. At present, there are
mainly three magnetic ﬁeld simulation methods: ﬁrst, the analytical method based
on the electromagnetic theoretical formula; second, the numerical analysis method
basedoncomputationalelectromagnetictheory;third,theequivalentmagneticsource
method using experimental data. The magnetism of any device can be seen as the
result of internal circuit and the magnetic material, so in theory, a magnetic model
can be established according to the classical electromagnetic theory based on the
conﬁguration, circuit layout, and magnetic material properties of the equipment, and
the magnetic distribution can be calculated. Most current electromagnetic analysis
software adopts this method, and uses ﬁnite element and ﬁnite difference methods
to perform magnetostatic modeling and simulation, especially for those equipment
with simple structure and clear material properties. However, for most equipment,
due to their complicated internal structure and unclear magnetic material properties,
it is difﬁcult to model and simulate with this method.
The equipment or instruments are composed of a large number of PCBs and
components, and the internal circuit is complicated. In addition, magnetic materials
may be used. Due to the complex properties of the magnetic materials, even for the
magnetic properties of the common materials such as iron and nickel, the magnetic
properties are difﬁcult to be accurately described, which makes the magnetic prop-
erties of the device more complicated. Even if the classical electromagnetic theory
described above is adopted, it is still very hard to perform magnetic modeling based
on materials and internal circuits and implement it from an engineering perspec-
tive. Therefore, it is necessary to simplify the magnetic properties of the equipment,
and represent the magnetic properties of the system with an equivalent simpliﬁed
magnetic model. Currently, the most common equivalent method is based on the
equivalent of a magnetic dipole.

9.5.1
Equipment Magnetic Design Procedures
For spacecraft with magnetic moment or magnetic ﬁeld control requirements, the
magnetic control documents shall be prepared according to the general requirements
for magnetic control, and the magnetic indexes of the components shall be rationally
allocated. The magnetic control procedures shall be strictly followed in the design
and development process.
9.5.1.1
Material Selection
When selecting materials at the beginning of the spacecraft design, materials with low
magnetizability should be selected as possible, and permanent magnetic materials
should be discarded under the comprehensive consideration of the physical properties
and use requirements of the material. Soft magnetic materials are susceptible to be
magnetized by the environmental magnetic ﬁeld, and may cause changes in the
magnetic moment and magnetic ﬁeld of the spacecraft, so care should be taken
to use soft magnetic materials. Materials and components of the same type and
speciﬁcations may have different magnetic properties. In type-I magnetic design, the
materials must be strictly detected and screened.

In spacecraft design, the number of magnetic sensitive instruments should be mini-

interference threshold. If necessary, an extension rod may be so designed that it
keeps a certain distance from the spacecraft to reduce the interference.
The component with lower magnetic properties is selected, not only permanent

9.5.1.3
Layout and Treatment
The rational layout of the materials and components in the spacecraft can eliminate
the magnetic properties between each other. The component layout design should
follow the following principles:
(1) For a number of components with certain remanence, it is recommended to
reversely install to enable mutual offset of the magnetic moment and the
magnetic ﬁeld, and minimize the total magnetic properties.
(2) Fasteners used on spacecraft such as nuts, bolts, screws, thread washers, and
gaskets should be made of nonmagnetic materials.
(3) Many discrete equipment contain nickel and lead, which may be highly magne-
tized. It is recommended to use nonmagnetic alloys, if necessary, the pin portion
should be cut as short as possible.
9.5.1.4
Magnetic Design of Current Loops
Controlling the current loop area and limiting the number of loops on the spacecraft is
the key to controlling the stray magnetic moments (magnetic ﬁelds) of the spacecraft.
The main principle is to reduce the loop area. The general requirements for wiring
design are as follows:
(1) Proper wiring inside and between the equipment can reduce the current loop
area and minimize the stray magnetic moments (magnetic ﬁelds). The current
loop greater than 10 mA inside the equipment should use twisted pairs as much
as possible, and the positive and return lines of the current loop with less than
10 mA should be placed as close as possible.
(2) Avoid forming a large area of current loop in the ground line.
(3) Use solenoid-shaped transformers and inductors and proper wiring to reduce
the leakage of the transformers and inductors.
(4) The battery cell pairs should be reversely mounted to offset the magnetic
moments (magnetic ﬁelds) generated by the loop current. When an odd number
of cells are used, an additional current loop should be used to offset the magnetic
moment (magnetic ﬁeld) which is generated by the cell.
(5) When the positive and negative terminals of a component need to be designed
separately, the trace of the single outer wire should follow the Principle of the
Shortest Path.
(6) In connector design, the lead wires should be twisted or bundled as early as
possible. For the pin layout, the positive and negative lines of the same load
current should be as close as possible.
(7) The solar array is quite special due to the existence of a large number of current
loops; the following considerations should be given to the wiring layout: First,
use the “8”-shaped trace for single-cell arrays to avoid the formation of a large
current loop, and mutual offset of the magnetic moments on the current loop of
adjacent modules; second, mutual offset the magnetic moments generated by the

9.5.1.5
Magnetic Shielding
If there are strong magnetic interference sources in the spacecraft, or some magnetic
sensitive components are very susceptible to other magnetic ﬁeld interference,
magnetic shielding may be considered. When a relay is not shielded, the magnetic
ﬂux at 300 mm is 259 nT, after shielding, it is reduced to 29 nT, a 78% reduction in
total.
When adopting magnetic shielding, the following should be paid attention to:
(a)
Metal magnetic materials as shields are only allowed to be used in DC magnetic
ﬁelds and LF magnetic ﬁelds. For HF magnetic ﬁeld, since the resistivity is
large and the eddy current is small, the demagnetization effect is relatively
poor. Generally, the frequency range of 0–300 Hz has a good shielding effect
on the interference magnetic ﬁelds;
(b) Since the magnetic properties of the spacecraft components are relatively low,
and the magnetic material for shielding is generally in the initial magnetization
state, shielding materials with high initial magnetic permeability and good AC
magnetic properties should be selected;
(c)
The magnetic shield opening has a great inﬂuence on the shielding effectiveness.
Therefore, when designing the magnetic shielding, the opening area should be
minimized to obtain the maximum SE;
(d) The magnetic shielding material itself is usually a soft magnetic material with
a certain remanence and strong inductive magnetics, so it should be strictly
evaluated during use.

9.6.2
Cable Layout Design
The components that can generate loop current in spacecraft mainly include trans-

The main methods to reduce loop current are as follows:
(1) Rational wiring in and between the equipment can reduce the loop current area
and minimize the stray magnetic moment generated by the loop current; when
designing the power supply line, twisted-pair cables should be used as much as
possible; the loop area of the solar array should be minimized, using backside
laying technique can effectively control the magnetic ﬁeld interference.
(2) The transformer and inductor leakage can be reduced by using a spiral tube
transformer and inductor as well as reasonable wiring.
(3) The operation of the battery cell may generate a magnetic moment by the loop
current between the electrodes. Therefore, when designing the battery pack, the
paired battery cells should be reversely installed to offset the magnetic moment
generated by the loop current. When an odd number of battery cells are required
to be used, it can be offset by a series of loop wires connected to the battery
cells.

magnetic current and magnetic ﬁeld interference due to the large current they carry

should be paid during the design phase.

General Requirements

(4) There should be no other unrelated equipment or magnetic object in the test

(5) It is better to avoid the periods of abnormal ﬂuctuation of the geomagnetic ﬁeld
and that of power consumption peak.

Magnetic Field Test
The spacecraft magnetic ﬁeld test is conducted in the uniform area of the zero
magnetic ﬁeld equipment. The test positions and the number of test points are deter-
mined by the test program. During the test, the magnetometer probe is placed at the
measurement points P1, P2, P3, P4, … as required in the program, as shown in
Fig. 9.23. The output value of the instrument reﬂects the magnetic ﬁeld characteris-
tics at each point. If the magnetic ﬂux is below 0.1 mT, a ﬂuxgate magnetometer is
usually used; if the magnetic ﬂux is above 0.1 mT, a Hall magnetometer is used.
9.7.2.4
Magnetic Moment Test
1. Overview of the methods
Generally, the spacecraft magnetic moment test methods include the dipole method,
equatorial method in a zero magnetic ﬁeld, and test in the geomagnetic ﬁeld.
The dipole method is used to estimate the magnetic moment, while the equatorial
method can accurately test the magnetic moment. In the absence of large magnetic
test equipment, the magnetic moment test can be done in the geomagnetic ﬁeld.

9.8
Summary
The magnetic design and test technology of the spacecraft have certain particu-
larity, which is manifested in relatively independent requirements and methods in
technical speciﬁcations, simulation methods, equipment-level magnetic design and
control, and whole-satellite magnetic design and control, and magnetic testing. With
the development and launch of electromagnetic measurement satellites, spacecraft
magnetic ﬁeld control has become a key factor to ensure the success of spacecraft
scientiﬁc exploration missions. In addition, new requirements are placed on related
design and test veriﬁcation.
