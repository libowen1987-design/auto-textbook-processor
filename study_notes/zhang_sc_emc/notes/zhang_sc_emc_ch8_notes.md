8.1.2
Design of Power Supply Filtering of the Equipment
The DC/DC conversion unit is the main source of conducted and radiated interfer-
ences in the equipment and its EMC design is also the focus of the EMC design for
the whole equipment. Special EMI ﬁlters, including a common-mode ﬁlter unit and
a differential mode ﬁlter unit, are designed at both the input and output ends of the
DC/DC converter.
The +5 and \pm12 V power required by the data acquisition unit is from the DC/DC
conversion unit. During application, two 0.1uf chip capacitors in series are set to
smooth the supply current and provide a low-resistance channel to the ground to
effectively ﬁlter out the noise mixed in the supplied power.
For the digital components used in the data acquisition unit, decoupling capac-
itors are added between their power module pins and the ground to eliminate the
interference current and to prevent the PCB board of the data acquisition unit from
resonating and affecting the normal operation of the unit.
A common-mode inductor is placed at the inlet of the 100 V input end of the
equipment to suppress the common-mode interference current. The external emission
interference (CE102 and RE102) of the equipment is mainly caused by common-
mode interference current. Therefore, the common-mode inductor is added to block
the transmission path of common-mode interference current, so as to avoid its trans-
mission to the outside of the equipment through the 100 V input line to cause the out-
of-limit phenomenon of CE102 and RE102. By placing the common-mode inductor
at the inlet of the 100 V input end, the common-mode interference generated by all
interference sources in the equipment can be suppressed, and the ﬁltering effect of
this inductor will not be weakened due to the spatial coupling effect of common-mode
interference signals that may bypass the inductor.
8.1.3
Shielding Design
The electromagnetic disturbance generated inside the equipment can emit radiation
through the apertures on the equipment shell. Likewise, external electromagnetic
disturbance will also enter the equipment through these apertures and interfere with
the sensitive circuit inside the equipment. When designing the shell of the power
supply and distribution equipment, the following measures can be taken to ensure its
good shielding efﬁciency:

(1) The shell material should be a good conductor with low resistivity;
(2) It is desired that no holes are exposed on all sides of the shell;
(3) The junction surfaces between shell structures should be as smooth as possible,
and should be non-blackened to reduce the contact resistance;
(4) Screwing and lapping should be combined as much as possible in the shell
structure design to control the contact resistance within 10 m;
(5) The junction surfaces of the equipment shell should be designed with concave
and convex grooves to block the electromagnetic radiation channels.
8.1.4
Grounding Design
The ideal “ground” would be a zero-resistance object with no potential difference
between the grounding points. However, in reality, such “ground” does not exist.
Any “ground” or “ground wire” has both resistance and electrical disturbance. When
current passes through, a voltage drop must occur so that zero potential will be impos-
sible. Actually, a ground voltage exists between two different grounding points.
Therefore, when each circuit is grounded at multiple points and when different
circuits communicate with each other through signals, an interference voltage will
be applied on the ground loop to generate a common-mode current in the signal line,
which will be superimposed on the useful signal and added to the load end. Due to
the unbalance of the circuit, the current in each wire is different. This current differ-
ence can even be converted into a differential-mode interference voltage, bringing
interference to the circuit.
In order to reduce the interference with the ground loop, the internal circuit board
should be designed to ensure that the signal ground wire is insulated from the shell
ground to form a ﬂoating ground. In other words, the return wire of the primary bus
is not connected to the structural ground in the equipment, but is grounded at the
power subsystem; and the return wire of the secondary power bus is connected to
the ground stud of the equipment at a single point. In order to realize single-point
grounding, the ground stud should be insulated from the equipment shell.

the interference of the whole equipment with the external environment, while other
items are mainly used for assessing the product’s sensitivity, namely the product’s
ability to withstand the interference injected from outside.

8.3.1
Composition and Electromagnetic Properties
of Integrated Electronic Products
In consideration of the developments and experiences of on-board integrated elec-
tronic products, we follow the concept of the modular design to divide the integrated
electronic system into different functional modules, for example, heater module,
power distribution module, telemetry and telecontrol modules, motor drive module,
pyrotechnics management module, and bus management module. According to the
requirements of the spacecraft, the service and payload compartments are combined
into a single device to implement the corresponding functions. The application of an
integrated electronic system reduces the number of electronic devices, the weight,
and effectively the number of cables in the spacecraft.
Based on the application of a modular structure, an internal bus chip that commu-
nicates between the bus management module and other modules is developed to
facilitate the bus management module to control other modules. As the management
core of the module, each internal bus chip is equipped with a crystal oscillator, whose
frequency is about 11.0592 MHz.
Taking integrated electronic products as an example, 10 modules are equipped in
the standard design, in which there are two bus management modules (one master and
one standby), two pyrotechnic management modules, one 7 V heater power distribu-
tion module, two 100 V hybrid power distribution modules, one analog acquisition
and discrete instruction module, one deployment antenna control module, and one
100Vheater module. Therearetotallyupto58external connectors withimplemented
interfaces including analog and digital data acquisition, double voltage acquisition,
pyrotechnics management interface, 100 V heater interface, 100 V power distribu-
tion interface, 7 V heater interface, 100 V power supply interface, analog data output
interface, motor interface, 1553B bus interface, CSB interface, ML/DS interface, 422
interface, UART interface, etc. It is obvious that the integrated service unit is char-
acterized in perspective of EMC by many interfaces, many cables, many leakages,
and many sources of transmission.

There are many external interfaces and many cables in the integrated service unit.

attenuator is added to the intermediate frequency part of the receiver to realize the
real temperature compensation.
The local oscillator link is mainly composed of a sampling phase locked-loop
circuit, a frequency doubling circuit, and a ﬁltering circuit. The external reference
signal is sampled and locked for output. The signal is locked to a certain frequency
through the sampling locked-phase frequency doubling circuit, then the signal is
multiplied to the local oscillation frequency required for frequency conversion, and
an ampliﬁer is used to level the power of the signal ampliﬁed into the range of power
level required by the local oscillator of the mixer.
The secondary power supply circuit supplies the secondary voltage required by
the RF link and the local oscillator link. This circuit controls the startup and shutdown
of the RF receiver according to the telecontrol command and provides telemetry of
“ON/OFF” status.

8.5.2.4
Radiation from Local Oscillator Circuit
The local oscillator circuit has many frequency components and high power. In the
test of RE102, the overproof frequencies are mostly local oscillator signals. The
following issues are to be taken into account to reduce the radiation of the local

(1) Design separate cavities for the devices with high power in the circuit or the

(2) Minimize the output power of devices in local oscillator circuit if the output
power is sufﬁcient. For example, the output level requirement of the CRO is
\geq5 dBm. The reasonable design is that the output power of the device is 5–6 dBm
other than 10 dBm, which is reduced to 5 dBm by the attenuation circuit.

8.5.3.1
Design Principles
The receiver should follow the EMC design principles listed below:
(1) Take system compatibility as the main purpose, rather than pursuing the best
single-machine index.
(2) The design of grounding and reasonable wiring are of great signiﬁcance.
The case of the receiver is provided with a grounding pile, and the internal
microstrip modules adopt the carrier welding process. The PCB also increases
the contact surface area with the case, thus ensuring the reliability. The signal
cable and the power cable are separated and routed according to the direction
of each high-frequency and low-frequency signal to avoid cross wiring.
(3) Combine structural design with electrical design to achieve a reasonable layout.
(4) The power cord follows the “reﬂow principle”.
(5) Pay attention to the electromagnetic compatibility within the product, and
strengthen the separation and shielding between the modules.
(6) Reduce the interference introduced by low-frequency socket and strengthen
power ﬁltering.
(7) In the design of the secondary power circuit board, the following contents should
be considered:
(a)
Components should have the characteristics of reducing noise and
suppressing interference.
(b) Pay attention to the setting of the grounding area on the PCB board and
the laying of the ground line.
(c)
Take measures to suppress common-mode interference.
